How to put your prices up without losing customers

A three-step plan, a letter you can copy, and what happened when 40 trades tried it this autumn.

Most of the trades we talk to have not changed their prices in two or three years. Fuel, materials and insurance have all gone up in that time, so the same job now pays noticeably less. Nobody enjoys the conversation, but it is far easier than people expect, and almost nobody leaves.

Why prices have to move

If your costs rise by 6% a year and your prices stay put, you are taking a pay cut every January. After three years, a job that used to leave you €100 might leave you €80. Customers know prices go up. What they do not like is a surprise.

Tip

Small, regular rises (once a year, in the quiet season) land far better than one large jump after five years of silence.

Step 1: know your numbers

Before you pick a number, work out what a typical job really costs you. Include the things that are easy to forget:

  • Travel time and fuel, not just time on site
  • Quoting time for the jobs you do not win
  • Insurance, phone, software and the van, spread over the year
  • Time spent chasing late payments

In Northwind, the Reports page shows your average job value and margin by service, which makes this a ten-minute job.

Step 2: give fair notice

Tell regular customers at least a month ahead, in writing, and keep it short. Here is a letter you can adapt:

From 1 January our prices will rise by 7%, the first change in two years. It covers higher fuel and material costs and lets us keep the same team and standards. Any job booked before then stays at today's price. Thank you for your custom.

Notice that it says what, when, why and what stays the same. It does not apologise, and it does not over-explain.

Step 3: show what they get

People pay for certainty. Remind them of the things that make you easy to deal with: you turn up when you say, you send a clear quote, you clean up after yourself. If you have added online booking, text reminders or a pay-now link, mention it.

What happened when 40 trades tried it

This autumn, 40 Northwind customers raised prices by between 5% and 12% using the steps above. We compared the three months before and after.

In short: income went up by about 8%, the number of jobs barely moved, and the conversations were, in the words of one roofer, "a lot less awkward than the dentist".

The short version

  1. Work out what a job really costs you, including travel and admin.
  2. Give a month's notice in writing: what, when, why, and what stays the same.
  3. Remind people why you are worth it, then get back to work.
