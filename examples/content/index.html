Prose

Most small business websites fail in the same quiet way. Nobody updates them. The opening hours drift, the gallery shows a van that was sold two years ago, and the contact form forwards to an inbox nobody reads.

A site that stays useful is built around the few things that change, and makes those things easy to change. Everything else can stay still. Read the full checklist before your next build.

What changes, and how often

Start by listing what a customer actually needs to know before they call. For a roofer, that is usually short:

  • Which towns you cover, and how quickly you can come out.
  • Recent jobs, with real photos and a line about each one.
  • A phone number that works on a phone, as a link.

The best website I ever had was the one I could update from the van between jobs.

A three-step update routine

  1. Take two photos at the end of every job.
  2. Upload the better one on Friday, with the town name.
  3. Check the opening-hours line once a month.

If the site has a feed for recent work, the markup can be as plain as this:

<li class="job">
  <img src="slate-roof-tuam.jpg" alt="Slate roof, Tuam">
  <p>Full re-slate, finished in four days.</p>
</li>
